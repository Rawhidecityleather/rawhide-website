Fixed Length vs Adjustable Radio Straps

RADIO STRAPS · 5 MIN READ

This is the first real fork in the road when you order a radio strap, and it gets argued about more than it deserves. Both work. They fail differently and they fit differently, and that's the whole conversation.

What each one actually is

Fixed length. Cut and assembled to one measurement. Usually a continuous loop with no adjustment hardware anywhere in the run of the strap. What you ordered is what you wear, permanently.

Adjustable. Built with hardware that lets you shorten or lengthen the strap inside a range. You still order a specific size range — adjustable doesn't mean one-size-fits-all — but you can move within it.

The case for fixed

  • Fewer parts. No adjustment hardware in the run of the strap means nothing there to work loose, wear out, or catch on anything.
  • Cleaner line. Nothing interrupting the leather. Some people just prefer how it looks.
  • Nothing to fiddle with. It's set. You never find it three inches off because it snagged getting off the rig.

Fixed makes the most sense if you've worn a strap for years, know your length cold, and wear roughly the same setup every shift.

The case for adjustable

  • Seasonal bulk. This is the big one. What you wear over a station uniform in July and what you wear over a coat in January are not the same length. Fixed splits the difference and is slightly wrong twice a year. Adjustable is right both times.
  • It absorbs a measuring mistake. Get it modestly wrong on a fixed strap and you have an expensive piece of leather that doesn't fit. Adjustable gives you room to recover.
  • Bodies change. Over the years a strap outlasts, that matters.
  • It's easier to hand down. Straps get passed to a probie or kept as a memorial piece. Adjustable travels better.

The objection, and whether it holds

The argument against adjustable is that the hardware is one more thing to fail, and that straps slip while you're wearing them.

The failure point is real in principle. In practice it comes down to what hardware got used. Heavy-duty snaps and Chicago screws both hold their setting under the weight of a portable radio — they're the same class of hardware that holds horse tack together. What slips is undersized hardware on thin leather, and that strap was going to disappoint you regardless of whether it adjusted.

If you're weighing this, don't ask "fixed or adjustable." Ask what hardware is in it and what happens to that hardware in a year. That question sorts good straps from bad ones far better than this whole debate does.

What we build, and why

Both of ours adjust, and both run on Chicago screws — a threaded post and cap you tighten down once and forget about. The Basic Adjustable Radio Strap stamps your text in one paint color. The Fully Custom Adjustable Radio Strap adds your stitch color, font, and text outline color.

We went that way because the seasonal bulk problem is the one nearly everyone hits, and because a strap someone can still wear after a measuring mistake is a better outcome for both of us than one they can't.

You still have to measure

Worth saying clearly, because it trips people up: adjustable is not infinitely adjustable. Ours are built in five ranges — 50"–56", 56"–62", 62"–68", 68"–74", 74"–80" — and the adjustment covers roughly six inches inside whichever range you pick.

Measure wrong and you land in the wrong range, and adjustment can't save you. Two methods, both quick, on the how to measure page.

Please note: there is a 50% of total purchase fee for any sizing adjustment needed because of an incorrect measurement. Measure twice.

Picking

  • Adjustable if you're buying your first strap, if your layers change with the season, or if you're buying it for someone else.
  • Fixed if you've worn one for years, know your number, and want the simplest possible object.

Most people should take adjustable. That's not a sales pitch — it's that the seasonal problem is real and the failure argument mostly isn't, provided the hardware is right.
