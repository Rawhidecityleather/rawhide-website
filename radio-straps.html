What to Look For in a Radio Strap

Leather weight. Thin leather rolls up on itself and stays rolled. Ours is 10–12 oz English bridle, thick enough to hold its shape with a portable hanging off it. It'll be stiff out of the box. That's the point — it wears in, not out.

What holds it together. The failure point on most straps isn't the leather, it's the hardware. Snaps take a beating and stay locked down. Chicago screws you tighten once and forget about. Either way, nothing on the strap should pop, slip, or work loose on a call.

The holster. A strap with no holster is half a strap. Both of ours ship with a black leather radio holder built to fit most portable radios, and an anti-sway strap so the radio isn't swinging into you every time you move.

Fit. A strap that's too long puts the radio somewhere you can't reach it in a hurry. Too short and it pulls across your neck. This is the one thing worth slowing down for — see below.

Getting the Length Right

Every strap is made to your measurement, so this is the part to get right the first time. Two minutes with a tape measure or a piece of string saves you weeks.

  1. Using a strap you already own — adjust it to the length you want, then measure from the top of one trigger snap to the top of the other. That's your overall length.
  2. Using string or webbing — start where you want the radio holster to sit. Run it across your body, over your shoulder, and back to where you started. That total is your overall length.

Straps are built in five ranges: 50"–56", 56"–62", 62"–68", 68"–74", and 74"–80". Pick the range your measurement lands in. Full walkthrough on the how to measure page.

Please note: there is a 50% of total purchase fee for any sizing adjustment needed because of an incorrect measurement. Measure twice.

Keeping It Alive

Leather that gets conditioned outlasts leather that doesn't. Wipe the grit off with a dry rag when you come off a run. When it starts looking dry, work a thin coat of leather butter in with a rag, let it sit, then buff it off. Thin coats more often beat one heavy coat. Keep it away from direct heat while it dries.

Do that and the strap darkens, softens, and takes the shape of the person wearing it. That's leather doing what it's supposed to do.

What's the difference between the two straps?

How long does a radio strap take to make?

How do you clean and care for a leather radio strap?

Dry rag for the grit. Conditioner when it looks dry — thin coat, rub it in, let it sit, buff it off. Keep it out of direct heat while it dries.
